Slow Light

Eight federal agencies drawn as constellations. Each star is a share of an agency's workforce, and its brightness is that share's years of experience. A cut puts a star out in a day. A new hire relights it faintly, and it brightens by one year per year. The faint points around each constellation are its people, one point per hundred.

  • This is a sketch, not a dataset. The figures are rough approximations from published reporting (GAO, Partnership for Public Service, Pew, agency announcements, and for ACF, tallies by former staff). Before you show it, check each one against OPM's workforce data and edit the EVENTS block in the source.
  • Each constellation has 12 stars, so one star is about 8% of that agency. Losses smaller than half a star don't put one out, which is why TSA and FAA (each down about 3%) stay whole. The bright stars joined by lines trace the figure. The looser stars around it belong to the same agency. Totals at Jan 2026: Forest Service, Park Service, CDC and IRS each down about 26–27%. TSA and FAA each down about 3%.
  • The lines between stars show positions. A line is drawn when both of its stars are filled, so the shape returns as soon as people are hired. Brightness shows experience, which returns one year at a time.
  • Experience per star is estimated from an assumed average tenure for each agency. Probationary firings remove the newest stars. Deferred resignations and early retirements remove the most experienced. RIFs pick stars at random.
  • Reinstated staff return at full brightness (the October 2025 RIFs, reversed in November; NIOSH reinstatements in January 2026). New hires, such as the IRS seasonal hires, return faint.
  • The ACF Social Services Block Grant team is the small separate triangle. Everyone with that expertise left, so it stays dark.
  • Shutdowns: while an agency is unfunded, its stars dim and turn cool, and a day counter runs under its name. Furloughed and unpaid staff are still employees, so no experience is lost and the stars return as they were when funding comes back. The exception is people who quit, like the more than 1,100 TSA officers who left during the 76-day DHS shutdown in 2026. Two shutdowns are shown: Oct 1 – Nov 12, 2025 (government-wide, 43 days) and Feb 14 – Apr 30, 2026 (DHS only). A four-day partial lapse from Jan 31 to Feb 3, 2026 is mentioned in a caption but not drawn.
  • The timeline ends at the present, October 2026. It shows no forecast. Funding is extended through Dec 11, 2026, so no third shutdown is drawn.
  • How changes show: when an event happens, the caption appears first. Then the stars of each agency it affects change one at a time, and a count of the people lost or returned ticks up under its name. A star that goes out flares and leaves an amber afterglow. A returning star arrives with a closing ring. New hires shift from blue to gold as they gain experience, and send out a small ring at every new year.
  • The workforce: the faint points around each constellation are its people, one point per 100 employees (about 2,800 in all). They follow the same events as the constellation stars, at a finer grain, so a loss of 1,100 people puts out 11 points. The meters and the percentages under each name are computed from these points.
  • Long exposure: shows Sep 2024 to Oct 2026 in a single image, like a star-trail photograph. The sky turns slowly, so each star draws a trail from where it was to where it is now. A trail's brightness is that star's experience at that moment. Trails that end in an amber dot are people who left; faint blue trails are new hires; blue stretches are shutdowns. The dial along the bottom reads the same turn of the sky as a calendar, with a tick for each event. Gallery mode closes every loop with it. Inspired by Aaron Koblin's Flight Patterns, where every line is one real flight.
  • Memory: press and hold "Remember Sep 2024" to see the sky as it was before the cuts. When you let go, amber rings briefly mark the experience that has not come back. Gallery mode does this on its own at the end of every loop.
  • Installation mode is for a room with a knob: nothing moves on its own. Turning the knob moves through time in either direction, and holding the space bar (or the knob's push button) remembers Sep 2024. The knob can send arrow keys, + and −, or scroll-wheel movement. After three minutes untouched it fades back to Sep 2024 for the next visitor. Press I to enter, or add #install to the address.
  • Gallery mode plays the full timeline on a loop, pausing on each event. Add #gallery to the end of the address to open straight into it.
