Slow Light

Eight federal agencies drawn as constellations. Each star is a share of an agency's workforce, and its brightness is that share's years of experience. A cut puts a star out in a day. A new hire relights it faintly, and it brightens by one year per year.

  • This is a sketch, not a dataset. The figures are rough approximations from published reporting (GAO, Partnership for Public Service, Pew, agency announcements, and for ACF, tallies by former staff). Before you show it, check each one against OPM's workforce data and edit the EVENTS block in the source.
  • Each constellation has 36 stars, so one star is about 2.8% of that agency. Totals at Jan 2026: Forest Service, Park Service, CDC and IRS each down about 26–27%. TSA down about 3%, FAA about 3%.
  • Experience per star is estimated from an assumed average tenure for each agency. Probationary firings remove the newest stars. Deferred resignations and early retirements remove the most experienced. RIFs pick stars at random.
  • Reinstated staff return at full brightness (the October 2025 RIFs, reversed in November; NIOSH reinstatements in January 2026). New hires, such as the IRS seasonal hires, return faint.
  • The ACF Social Services Block Grant team is the small separate cluster. Everyone with that expertise left, so it stays dark even when every other vacancy is refilled.
  • "Refill every vacancy" is hypothetical. It shows how long full recovery would take in the best case: every empty position filled by July 2026, with no further cuts.
